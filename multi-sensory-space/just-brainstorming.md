# Just Brainstorming

Some thoughts:
- Play with scale: make it so that you feel like a small creature (door mouse, frog, etc.) in a big forest.
- Bring in piney, earthy scents. - pine needles could be 'activated' by being under the floor and being crushed when the person comes in.
- I think an important part of the connection is kneeling - I got on my knees and sprawled on my stomach to fully experience the moss from its level - rather than observing coldly and distantly from above. Japanese traditional tea houses have entrances into the tea chambers that are only half-hight. This has an affordance for kneeling and entering on one's knees, and an anti-affordance for walking in while standing up.
- Texture and "spring" of the surfaces are important - creating a softness that you can collapse into, but that rebounds as you move away. 
    - memory foam underneath soft cloth?
- I had a desire to drink in the moss/forest flavour. I will have a method to dispense hot tea in the space for people to drink.
    - hot tea faucet - tea is herbal so can steep very long and be pre-made in large batches. Grove of lichen with a dozen lichen-shaped cups. Nook for you to leave your used cup in...
    - How to communicate non-visually that the tea is herbal and on offer?
    How to give signifiers to the affordance of dispensing tea from the spigot?
    - How to demonstrate what cups to use and where to put them when they are done?
    -  Also, if you were a small creature in the woods, you might like a snack. Candied ginger? Ginger cookies? Pine needle cookies? Offering cookies would be a good signifier that the tea is on offer too.
    - What about communicating allergens? Audio only? What about other languages?
