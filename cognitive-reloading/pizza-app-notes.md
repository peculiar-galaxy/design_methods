## Analysis

What are we offloading when we only cook by strictly following recipes?
- process memory
- understanding of the purpose of ingredients: what can be substituted, what flavors go togther, etc. 
- intuitive measuring (just taking a pinch of salt vs needing to use a teaspoon every time)
- using our sensory awareness (smelling, hearing, seeing, feeling, tasting when something is ready rather than relying on a timer or thermometer)


What are some reasons someone want to learn to cook pizza without relying on a recipe?
- to be able to adapt to what they have in their pantry
- to be able to adapt the paramaters (toping selection, sauce flavor profile, crust thickness, doneness, etc.) to their personal taste.
- to be able to cook more quickly and confidently.
- to be able to be social while cooking unstead of burried in a recipe: pizza making parties!
- to be able to teach their friends to make pizza.
- for the joy of it!

First-Time Instructions:
- Proofing yeast (picture)
     - warm water (30 sec microwave, nice bathwater, can leave finger in without it getting too hot)
     - add yeast and water to large bowl
     - wait 5 mins
     - check if yeast has bubbled
     - if nothing, add more yeast, wait longer? Or you may have bad yeast.
- Kneading dough (picture)
     - add salt, oil, and flour. 
     - mix by hand or mixer
     - knead for 5 min by mixer, 10 min by hand.
- Proofing dough (picture)
     - add a lil more oil to the bowl.
     - pull the dough into a ball and place in the bowl
     - cover and leave somewhere warm for a few hours or until doubled
