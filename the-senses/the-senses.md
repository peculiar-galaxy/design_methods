# The Senses
A reflection on The Wondersmith's Geode Potatoes

Last year, after quitting my job, I moved cross|country to apprentice with a ceramics artist. My mentor went by the title 'The Wondersmith', and she designed emmersive experiences involving ceramic vessels, tea, food, costumes, and embodied ritual. While I was apprenticed with her, I supported one of her events | a dinner party hosted in a lava tube cave designed around the theme of surprise.

One of the courses in the dinner was a dish she designed called Geode Potatoes. To make it, brightly colored root vegtables (golden and red beets, purple, yellow, and blue potatoes) are coated in olive oil and individually wrapped in thimbleberry leaves with salt and herbs. Then, they are wrapped in a cennimeter layer of clay, rolled in charcoal powder, and baked in the oven. The clay hardens, and the vegtables look like...ordinary rocks. 

The rocks were dispersed on the table, and when it came time to eat, guests, to their delight, were given rock hammers, and began to crack open the clay to reveal vibrant, vegtable "geodes" inside. Romesco sauce was spooned over the geode innards as they were devoured...

This dish was designed with all the senses: the cacophany of fourteen hammers attacking fake rocks in an echoey cave, the tactile exploration of plucking peices of steaming hot, tender vegatable from the hard shells, the visual punch of ruby, violet, and marigold vegatables emerging from the black stones, the complex aroma of herbs and charcoal, and the taste of creamy, tangy romesco sauce.

I was most curious about smell, and wanted to categorize the scents that made up the experience of this unusual dish with some of the scent "borrowed language" from the reading:
